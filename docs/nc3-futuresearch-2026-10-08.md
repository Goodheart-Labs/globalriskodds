## NC3 hack confirmed or reported by end of 2030 (the headline)

**Effort:** high · **Probability:** 13%

**Question:** Between 8 October 2026 and 31 December 2030, will the government of any nuclear-armed state publicly confirm, OR at least one major outlet (Reuters, AP, New York Times, Washington Post, Wall Street Journal, Financial Times, BBC, The Guardian) report citing current or former officials, that a cyber intrusion compromised any component of a nuclear weapons command, control and communications (NC3) system of any nuclear-armed state, including early-warning, launch-authorisation, or nuclear-forces communications systems, or that such a component was compromised via its supply chain? Intrusions limited to unclassified administrative networks of nuclear agencies, or to civil nuclear power, do not count.

The probability of a publicly confirmed or reported cyber intrusion into a nuclear weapons command, control, and communications (NC3) system stands at 13%. The bar for this event is exceptionally high: it requires either direct official confirmation by a nuclear-armed state or reporting by specific major outlets citing officials. Crucially, the compromise must affect actual NC3 components—such as early warning, launch authorization, or nuclear-forces communications—or their supply chains. Breaches of unclassified administrative networks, research labs, or civil nuclear facilities explicitly do not qualify.

The historical baseline for such public disclosures is functionally zero. Previous high-profile "nuclear hacks" have consistently fallen short of the criteria because they were limited to business or unclassified IT networks. For example, during both the 2020 SolarWinds incident and the 2025 SharePoint breach affecting the US National Nuclear Security Administration, officials confirmed that the malware was isolated to administrative networks and no mission-essential NC3 functions were compromised [https://www.washingtontimes.com/news/2021/jan/5/top-admiral-solarwinds-computer-hack-didnt-harm-us/](https://www.washingtontimes.com/news/2021/jan/5/top-admiral-solarwinds-computer-hack-didnt-harm-us/) [U.S. Energy Department probes hack, says no threat to national ...](https://www.reuters.com/business/us-energy-department-says-cyber-hack-limited-business-networks-2020-12-17/) [US energy department confirms it was hit by Sunburst hack](https://www.bbc.com/news/world-us-canada-55358332) [Foreign hackers breached a US nuclear weapons plant via ...](https://www.csoonline.com/article/4074962/foreign-hackers-breached-a-us-nuclear-weapons-plant-via-sharepoint-flaws.html) [US nuclear weapons agency breached in Microsoft ...](https://www.reuters.com/world/us/us-nuclear-weapons-agency-breached-microsoft-sharepoint-hack-bloomberg-news-2025-07-23/) [US nuclear weapons agency breached in Microsoft ...](https://www.reuters.com/world/us/us-nuclear-weapons-agency-breached-microsoft-sharepoint-hack-bloomberg-news-2025-07-23/). Similarly, a 2012 Chinese intrusion into the White House Military Office only affected an isolated, unclassified network [Chinese Hackers Take Bad Trip to White House; Nuclear ...](https://www.bitdefender.com/en-us/blog/hotforsecurity/chinese-hackers-take-bad-trip-to-white-house-nuclear-command-system-among-targets). 

Despite the strong precedent of operational secrecy and system air-gapping, several structural factors elevate the risk over the multi-year window. The timeframe covers all nine nuclear-armed states, offering a broad target area. Furthermore, US government materials acknowledge that as legacy NC3 systems modernize and migrate toward internet protocols, their cyber exposure naturally accelerates [Nuclear Weapons Employment Policy, Planning, and NC3](https://www.acq.osd.mil/ncbdp/nm/NMHB2020rev/chapters/chapter2.html). Elite concern over this vulnerability is tangible: US statutory mechanisms mandate quarterly congressional briefings on NC3 cyber-resiliency [U.S.C. Title 10 - ARMED FORCES](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title10/html/USCODE-2024-title10-subtitleA-partI-chap24-sec499.htm), the Defense Department launched a 2026 audit of high-risk NC3 cyber vulnerabilities [Audit of Nuclear Command, Control, and Communications ...](https://www.dodig.mil/Reports/Audits-and-Evaluations/Article/4384894/project-announcement-audit-of-nuclear-command-control-and-communications-cybers/) [Audit of Nuclear Command, Control, and Communications ...](https://www.dodig.mil/reports.html/Article/4384894/project-announcement-audit-of-nuclear-command-control-and-communications-cybers/) [Audit of Nuclear Command, Control, and Communications ...](https://www.dodig.mil/reports.html/Article/4384894/project-announcement-audit-of-nuclear-command-control-and-communications-cybers/), and US-Chinese expert dialogues have discussed establishing red lines against autonomous attacks on nuclear command systems [US, China security experts propose nuclear-style ...](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/us-china-security-experts-propose-nuclear-style-safeguards-ai-risks-2026-09-17/) [US, China security experts propose nuclear-style ...](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/us-china-security-experts-propose-nuclear-style-safeguards-ai-risks-2026-09-17/). 

The most plausible pathways to a qualifying public disclosure involve supply-chain discoveries or wartime leaks rather than direct operational hacks. The explicit inclusion of "supply chain" vulnerabilities provides a realistic route for resolution; for instance, US officials might leak the discovery of compromised foreign hardware or software in early-warning or satellite communications systems to justify defensive measures or sanctions. Additionally, ongoing geopolitical conflicts create openings for edge cases, such as an officially sourced Ukrainian intelligence claim about hacking Russian nuclear-forces communications being relayed by Reuters or the BBC [Ukrainian Hackers Obtained Data on Russia's Nuclear ...](https://militarnyi.com/en/news/ukr-hackers-data-on-russia-nuclear-forces/) [Ukraine's military intelligence claims cyberattack on ...](https://therecord.media/ukraine-military-russia-strategic-bomber).

Ultimately, the low probability reflects the immense structural and political barriers to public confirmation. Governments possess overwhelming strategic incentives to keep NC3 vulnerabilities highly classified, both to maintain deterrence stability and to avoid exposing their own offensive cyber capabilities. Even if a genuine NC3 or supply-chain compromise occurs, the combined requirements of discovery, official attribution, and specific public reporting make it highly unlikely that such an event will be broadcast to the world before the end of 2030.

While a pivot away from public leak sites or a cascading ecosystem collapse remains a plausible tail risk, current evidence suggests diminished monetization has triggered higher attack volumes rather than a retreat. Weighing the massive mathematical hurdle required against the long-term horizon and shifting extortion economics, a 20% annual decline remains a severe outlier scenario, finalized at the 11% threshold.

(f) Bets: A base rate near zero over decades suggests a very low probability, but accounting for the "safety-system function" clause and rising geopolitical tensions, I'm comfortable with an 9% probability.
